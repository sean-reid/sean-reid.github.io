Sounds

A voxel world where the music is generated from the biome you're standing in.

May 2026

Live Demo Source

Walk through a generated voxel world and everything you hear is composed on the spot from where you are. In a temperate forest an Aeolian lead plays over jazz-voiced pad chords. In the dunes the scale flips to Phrygian dominant with a flamenco bass line. In deep ocean everything slows to a Lydian drone. In a mushroom biome you're in half-whole diminished over electronic percussion. I built it because I wanted a world where the terrain is the score, and the voxel game around it is only there to give you somewhere to walk.

The world

Terrain is three octaves of simplex noise at scales 0.005, 0.015, and 0.04, weighted 0.6, 0.25, and 0.15. Separate temperature and moisture fields pick one of 20 biomes, from deep ocean and coral reef through mangrove, bayou, savanna, dunes, mushroom, and alpine meadow, up to glacial peaks and frozen tundra. Height at a biome border is blended with a smoothstep over a 16-block grid so there are no cliffs where the climate changes. Chunks are 16 blocks square and load in a ring around the player. There are three device profiles, and the only real difference is shadows: 2048-pixel shadow maps on desktop, 1024 on tablet, none on phones.

Seven voices

The music engine runs seven Tone.js voices on their own schedules. The lead plays phrases of three to eight notes from the current biome's scale, biased by where you look. Look up and the melody trends higher. The pad plays FM chords on two-bar cycles. The bass has ten strategies assigned by biome: walking, dub, ostinato, bossa nova, afrobeat, blues, flamenco, drone, spectral, and industrial. Percussion synthesizes eight drum sounds from scratch and layers them into a kit per biome. An ambient voice runs brown and pink noise through bandpass filters, with shimmer near water and rumble underground. An arpeggiator has five patterns. And a ping voice plays a sine tone with a long reverb tail when you click, which is the only sound you control directly. Everything runs through a 4-second reverb at 0.3 wet, a feedback delay, and a limiter at -3 dB. Nothing is sequenced from pre-written bars. Each voice decides what to play every beat.

A harmony engine keeps the voices agreeing. It tracks the current chord, its tensions and avoid notes, and a tension value that rises and falls over a progression, and the lead, pad, bass, and arp all take their notes from it. The progression library has 34 entries: modal vamps, ii-V-I cadences, blues shuffles, bossa nova, afrobeat, flamenco Phrygian cadences, drones, gamelan cycles, chromatic sequences, raga pedal tones. The scale system has 35 scales, from pentatonic major and Dorian to slendro, iwato, and Bhairav. Each biome picks a scale, a tempo range, a bass strategy, a kit, an arp pattern, and a progression style on top of one of eight genre presets.

The rhythm library holds 17 patterns transcribed from the clave family and its relatives: son and rumba clave, tresillo, cinquillo, bossa nova, and then habanera, agbadza, bembe, gahu, cascara, and songo. At least two of those are wrong. My habanera has hits on pulses 0, 3, and 5 of eight, and the real one is 0, 3, 4, 6. My bembe drops the twelfth pulse and comes out as a variant of the agbadza bell sitting right above it in the file. Nothing in the tests checks any music theory, which is how they got through. Timing is humanized by up to 5 ms either side of the grid, and a kit is held for 64 steps before the engine will consider switching.

Crossing a border

When you walk from one biome into another the parameters don't snap. The engine samples the biomes in a 32-block radius around the player at 8-block steps, weights each sample by distance, and normalizes the weights to sum to one, so the music parameters are a weighted mix of the biomes nearby. Then each parameter is smoothed exponentially with its own time constant: 8 seconds for the biome mix, 4 for elevation, 2 for movement speed, 1 for camera pitch. You hear the gradient as you cross it, which is the effect the whole thing is built around.

Time

A ten-minute day cycle drives a second layer of change. At night the reverb wet mix ramps from 0.3 to 0.45, the arpeggiator loses 4 dB, and the pad gains 3. By day the lead is 4 dB louder than at night. The same forest at midnight and at noon has the same scale, the same bass, and a different mix. On top of that, the engine cycles through four sections, ambient for 16 bars, building for 12, full for 20, and breakdown for 8, each with its own gain targets per voice and a two-second ramp between them. Biomes give the music spatial variation. The sections give it variation while you stand still.

Controls are WASD and mouse on desktop, a virtual joystick and touch-look on phones, and click or two-finger tap to ping. There's no objective. There's also no README, and the only two Playwright tests check that the world loads on desktop and on a phone viewport.
