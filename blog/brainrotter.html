BrainRotter

Fills a page with grammatical brainrot from a weighted grammar and a 2,277-term lexicon.

March 2026

Live Demo Source

The internet has a dialect. "Skibidi," "rizz," "sigma," and "sus" have their own grammar and usage, opaque to anyone over 25, and I wanted to see what happened when I fed that vocabulary into a proper context-free grammar. Press the button and BrainRotter fills the page with paragraphs of grammatically structured, completely unhinged brainrot.

The grammar

The engine is a weighted context-free grammar in the style of SCIgen, 910 lines of plain JavaScript. There are 22 weighted sentence types: simple and compound clauses, relative clauses on the subject or the object, nested and compound-complex constructions, conditionals, comparatives, concessives, temporals, three kinds of question, dialogue, meta-commentary, escalation, negation, fake academic citations, enumerations, and callbacks. Simple and compound sentences carry weights of 10 and 9 and enumerations get 2, so the plain structures dominate and the elaborate ones show up often enough to keep the output from looping.

The lexicon is 2,277 terms in 13 categories: 419 entities (Skibidi Toilet, Bombardiro Crocodilo, Kai Cenat, Quandale Dingle), 214 transitive and 224 intransitive verbs ("rizzes up," "mogs," "is locked in"), 366 adjectives ("goated," "skibidi-coded," "looksmaxxed"), and then adverbs, prepositional phrases, interjections, and the small categories that glue sentences together. It runs from Among Us through the Italian brainrot era.

Morphology

The fiddly part was English morphology. Verbs in the lexicon are stored in the third-person present ("rizzes up") because that's how most sentences use them, but a question needs do-support and the bare form. "Skibidi Toilet rizzes up the sigma" has to become "Why did Skibidi Toilet rizz up the sigma?" So deconjugateTV() picks a transitive verb, drops any 's or 'd contraction, finds the first word ending in s, and strips it back with the usual rules: "ies" to "y", "es" to the base unless the stem needs its e, and a plain "s" off unless the word ends in "ss" or "us". Intransitives that start with "is" lose the "is" for the "Is X bussin?" question form. Commas go before a coordinating conjunction between independent clauses and after a leading dependent clause. Those details are the difference between sentences and word salad.

A cast and a place

Before generating, the engine picks 5 to 8 cast members from the entity pool and 3 recurring locations from the prepositional phrases. Characters are tracked as they're introduced, with a bias toward the cast that eases off as more of them have appeared and a rule against reusing anyone from the last two sentences, so the generator can write callbacks ("Meanwhile, Skibidi Toilet was still...") and consequences that refer to earlier events. Paragraphs come from seven archetypes (opening, narrative, academic, interrogative, dramatic, comparison, freeform), each with its own pool of sentence types, and recent types are remembered so the same construction doesn't land twice in a row.

Filling the box

The output box has a fixed height and I wanted it exactly full. The generator asks for 30 paragraphs, then adds them one at a time to a hidden div styled to match the box, checking scrollHeight against the box's height after each and stopping before the one that overflows. If there's still room it generates 20 more and keeps going. The text regenerates on resize after a 200 ms pause, and Space or Enter does the same as the button.

It's vanilla HTML, CSS, and JavaScript with ES modules, loaded straight by the browser. There are PWA icons and a manifest because people were adding it to their home screens, and there are no tests.
