Tongue Simulator

Type a sentence and watch a simulated tongue, jaw, and velum move through it while the browser speaks.

Live Demo Source

The tongue is a muscular hydrostat with no skeleton, and during speech it moves through a sequence of shapes most people never see. Type some text, press Speak, and the browser reads it aloud while drawing a midsagittal cross-section of the vocal tract, with the tongue, jaw, velum, and lips moving through the articulation for each sound in time with the audio.

From text to targets

Text becomes ARPAbet phonemes through a 427-word dictionary pulled from CMUdict, with a rule-based fallback for anything else that handles digraphs, silent letters, and the magic-e pattern. Each phoneme has an articulatory target, an 18-parameter state: nine muscle activations (anterior and posterior genioglossus, styloglossus, hyoglossus, palatoglossus, superior and inferior longitudinal, transverse, and vertical), plus jaw angle, lip protrusion, rounding, and spread, velum angle, hyoid offset, glottal aperture, and voicing. A gestural score in the sense of Browman and Goldstein's articulatory phonology turns the phoneme timeline into a continuous trajectory, blending each phoneme's target with its neighbors so the tongue is already moving toward the next sound before the current one ends.

The tongue as a soft body

The tongue mesh is two chains of 15 nodes, a dorsal surface and a ventral one, with the root pinned. Distance constraints run along and between the chains, and each quad between neighboring node pairs carries an area constraint meant to keep the tongue's volume constant as it deforms, which is what a hydrostat does. The solver is position-based dynamics after Müller et al. (2007): predict positions from velocities and muscle forces, project the constraints ten times, then derive velocities from the corrected positions. It runs at 240 Hz. The jaw, velum, hyoid, and lips are rigid bodies driven by PD controllers toward their targets, and a collision pass pushes the tongue off the palate.

The area constraint has a bug. The gradient it uses has the wrong sign for two of the four corners relative to how the area is computed, so on each quad two corners push to expand while two push to shrink, and the volume preservation I meant to have is not enforced. The distance constraints and the palate collision hold the shape well enough that the animation still reads as a tongue, which is why it doesn't show. The tests only check that nothing goes non-finite and that the nodes stay inside a loose box, and they wouldn't have caught it.

Precompute, then play

The simulation is precomputed rather than run live. Pressing Speak runs the whole PBD simulation for the utterance plus half a second of return to rest, after 50 warm-up steps at the rest pose, and stores a snapshot per 240 Hz frame. Playback interpolates between snapshots, so the physics can cost whatever it costs without dropping frames. The hard part was the audio. The Web Speech API gives no timeline, only word boundary events, so each boundary becomes an anchor pairing wall-clock time with simulation time, and between boundaries the playhead advances at the stretch ratio measured on the previous word. Before the first boundary it assumes speech runs 1.6 times slower than the simulation's phoneme durations. It adapts within one word.

The Rust crate compiles to WebAssembly with wasm-bindgen and the frontend is React with a canvas renderer. There's also an airflow module with a 44-station area function and a particle system that nothing calls. Twelve tests cover the physics and the g2p, and CI only deploys.
