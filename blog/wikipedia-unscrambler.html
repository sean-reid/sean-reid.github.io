Wikipedia Unscrambler

A word game that scrambles a passage from one of yesterday's most-read Wikipedia articles

Live Demo Source

The typoglycemia meme says you can read jumbled text as long as the first and last letters stay put. That's an overstatement, but context clearly does a lot of the work when you read, and I wanted a game that measured how much. So this scrambles every letter of every word, first and last included, and gives you nothing but the words around it.

The game fetches a real article, cuts out four sentences, and shuffles each word in place with punctuation left where it was. You type your guess for each word into its slot and it locks green when you're right. Separately, you can guess the article title at any point. The two tasks feed each other: a few unscrambled words tell you the topic, and the topic tells you what the remaining words could be. Three hints are available for the title, in order the character count, the first letter, and the first word.

Yesterday's top 500 as a difficulty dial

Not all articles are equally guessable. The United States has vocabulary anyone knows. A particular species of moth does not. The one idea in this project is to use Wikimedia's pageview API as the difficulty signal: I pull yesterday's 500 most-viewed articles on English Wikipedia, drop the Main Page and anything with a namespace prefix, and slice by rank. Easy is the top 50, medium is 50 to 200, hard is 200 to 500. Easy comes out as countries, celebrities, and whatever was in the news. Hard is articles I've never opened.

Because the ranking is a day old, the puzzles rotate on their own. Someone who trends on a Tuesday is an easy puzzle on Wednesday and gone by Thursday. I never have to curate a list.

A few small choices matter more than they look. If the article has more than ten sentences I skip the first three to six before taking my four, because the lede usually names the subject outright. Extracts under 200 characters get thrown away and another article drawn. All three difficulties are fetched at once and keep their own state, so switching from easy to hard doesn't lose the easy puzzle.

The whole thing is one 610-line HTML file with two commits. The pageview and extract APIs both allow cross-origin requests, so there's nothing for a server to do.
